# Record Screen — Design Inspiration

The record screen is the active recording experience — what the user sees after tapping the record button and while they're speaking. The design goal is a blend of **voice capture** (functional elements: waveform, elapsed time) and **meditation timer** (aesthetic tone: calm, minimal, contemplative).

---

## Cleft — Active voice recording

- **cleft-recording.png** — Active recording screen from Cleft (#1 Voice Notes App). A 3D wireframe mesh sphere animates in the center of the screen, reacting to audio input — it serves as both a visual confirmation of recording and an ambient, almost meditative focal point. Below: a prompt ("What ideas do you want to share?"), elapsed/max time (0:09 - 5:00), and a red stop button. Top bar has just close (X), title ("Untitled"), and Pause. The screen is spacious — the 3D visualization dominates the middle third, with generous whitespace above and below.

**What to take**: The 3D reactive visualization as an alternative to a flat waveform — it gives the recording screen a sense of life and presence without being distracting. The prompt text that gently frames the recording as a creative act. The elapsed/max time format. The overall spaciousness.

## Apple Voice Memos — Active recording state

- **voice-memos-record.png** — The canonical "recording in progress" screen. Dark background, red waveform scrolling left-to-right as audio is captured, large elapsed time display (00:05.79), pause button in a pill-shaped track, and a "Done" text button. Minimal chrome — the waveform and timer dominate.

**What to take**: The waveform-as-heartbeat visual feedback that confirms "yes, it's recording." The large, legible elapsed time. The pause/done control simplicity.

## Oak — Active breathing session

- **oak-3.jpg** — A large centered circle with "Exhale" text, a subtle progress bar at the bottom, and nothing else. The screen is almost entirely empty — just the breathing prompt and the circle animation. This is the gold standard for "calm active session" UI.

**What to take**: The radical minimalism of the active session screen — one central visual element (circle/animation), one piece of text, a progress indicator, and nothing else. The idea that during an active session, the screen should feel spacious and unhurried.
